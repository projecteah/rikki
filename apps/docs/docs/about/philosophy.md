# Philosophy

I spent some time thinking about what principles this tool should follow. The ideas below help me clarify trade-offs, and hopefully help you understand what makes Rikki different.

## Notes You Truly Own

Imagine the largest cloud provider suddenly going down, or a software you have used for years turning malicious without a way to export your full data. These things have actually happened.

I want to record my thoughts immediately without worrying about the current network condition, and I do not want my data locked into any single vendor's ecosystem. This is why I chose a "local-first, online sync" approach to manage notes. You can still write quickly while offline, and sync to other devices when connected.

## Affordable Self-Hosting

In the past, I often saw great self-deployable software but lacked a stable VPS to deploy it on. Cloud service providers like Cloudflare offer generous free tiers that are more than enough for personal users to leverage their complete and high-availability infrastructure, which is usually more than sufficient for writing notes.

Naturally, self-hosting remains optional. You can still enjoy the local version fully.

## Open Source

When code is fully public, you can check for yourself whether the application secretly uploads your privacy, also have the community take over if I can no longer maintain it, so your notes can continue to keep you company safely.

## No AI Writing

I am not entirely against AI. On the contrary, AI can use your notes as context to better assist you in reasoning and reflecting, exploring ideas you have not thought.

However, I do not plan to add features that use AI to edit or polish your text, now or in the future. Once AI intervene in sentence construction and polishing, those slightly rough but sincere thoughts can easily be smoothed away, and you may no longer be sure which words truly belong to you.

In this era of ubiquitous AI, it is precious to remain yourself and your words.

## Open Your Notes

After syncing, you can set some notes to private or public, similar to a microblogging platform.

I also provide an open API that you can seamlessly embed into your personal website. If you deploy the web version, you can immediately get a public page without much extra configuration.

This way you can easily publish your thoughts on something, a fun personal experience, or your current situation to provide reference and help for those who need it.

## No Social Features

I do not plan to include likes or comments, nor do I intend to build a small forum or social platform. This creates a minimal writing environment to avoid unnecessary distractions, which often makes it easier to express yourself freely.

If you want people to like or comment on your notes, you can simply share the public link to your preferred social platform or friends and family who might reply.

If someone finds it on the internet and is interested, they can also contact you through the email or social media link you leave behind.

## Low Mental Burden

I try to reduce the pressure of organizing language and considering consequences when writing notes through short forms and containers, avoiding giving up when trying to maintain the habit, and sustaining the practice into the more distant future.
